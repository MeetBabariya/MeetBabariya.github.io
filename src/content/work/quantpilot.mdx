## The wedge

Trading front-ends on Hyperliquid earn **builder fees** on the volume they route. The leaders are Telegram bots and copy-trading apps. When I studied them, they shared three blind spots:

1. **Leaderboards sort by raw P&L**, which rewards leverage and luck.
2. **Custody fear**: many bots hold user keys.
3. **No AI layer** to explain what a trader is actually doing.

QuantPilot targets all three, and charges 0.03% instead of the 0.05% an incumbent charges.

## Ranking that survives luck

A trader with a great month is usually lucky. The ranking worker scores accounts on **risk-adjusted and funding-adjusted** returns (perp funding can quietly dominate P&L), then applies a **Deflated Sharpe Ratio** filter. That corrects the Sharpe for how many candidates were screened and for non-normal returns, so the top of the board is less likely to be the best of many coin flips. The worker discovers accounts and has run on live mainnet data.

I started in Python and moved the product surface to TypeScript deliberately. Builder revenue scales with **routed volume, not latency**, so distribution and UX matter more than raw speed. A single TS stack (bot, web, API) on a mature Hyperliquid SDK ships faster. The quant code stayed in Python, where it belongs, and both languages share one PostgreSQL schema. I verified that integration end to end.

## Where it stands

- **Built:** onboarding and signing, execution with builder code (verified on testnet), and the ranking engine (run on mainnet data).
- **Next:** the AI copilot. It explains a trader's style, exposure and drawdowns in plain language before you copy them.
