## Why

Ask an LLM about one company's latest filing and it will answer confidently from memory. In finance a wrong figure is worse than no figure. Retrieval-augmented generation fixes that by **retrieving the relevant text first** and making the model answer only from it.

## Design decisions

- **Overlapping character windows.** Filings bury key numbers mid-paragraph. A 200-character overlap stops a figure and its label from landing in different chunks.
- **Local embeddings.** `sentence-transformers` runs on a laptop, so indexing a 100-page filing costs nothing and keeps the documents on the machine.
- **Refusal is a feature.** The prompt restricts the model to the retrieved passages and tells it to say when they don't contain the answer. A cited "not stated in the filing" beats a plausible guess.
- **Citations by construction.** Every passage goes into the prompt with an index, and the answer must reference `[n]`. A reader can check every claim in one click.

## What I'd add next

The next step is to measure it, not just demo it: a small labelled question set per filing to track **retrieval recall@k** and **answer faithfulness**; hybrid BM25 + vector retrieval for exact line items like "diluted EPS"; and table-aware parsing, because many financial facts live in tables that character windows split badly.
